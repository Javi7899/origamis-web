How to learn origami from scratch: where to start

Which paper to use, the folds and bases to master in order, and the first models that work the first time.

The paper

15 × 15 cm origami paper, thin and coloured on one side, so you can see which side is up. If you have none, cut printer paper into a square: fold one corner to the opposite edge and cut off the leftover strip.

The two folds behind everything

Valley (the paper folds towards you, making a V) and mountain (away from you). Every diagram is written with them. Crease each fold with your fingernail: soft creases are why most models will not close.

The bases, in order

1. Kite base: planes and first animals. 2. Blintz base (four corners to the centre): the masu box and the fortune teller. 3. Reverse, squash and petal folds. 4. Preliminary base and waterbomb: the crane and the flapping bird come from them. 5. Modular, boxes and tessellations. 6. Crease patterns and designing your own models.

First models that work

The dart plane, the heart, the tulip, the simple dog, the jumping frog and the masu box. They take under ten minutes and teach the folds you will need later.

Beginner mistakes

Not lining up the corners before creasing, skipping the pre-crease, and following a diagram without looking at the next step (it usually explains the current one). If you get stuck, unfold and fold again: the paper remembers the good creases.

How Origamis helps

Origamis teaches in this order in a 24-month course. Every fold is animated in 3D with the arrows of classic diagrams, and you can turn the model or watch it in slow motion. Models you know come back in review so you do not forget them. The first month is free.
